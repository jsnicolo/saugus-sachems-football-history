J.W. Stanley · 1901

Stanley coached the earliest Saugus High team in the season archive. Three surviving road-game reports put names and positions to his 1901 lineup. Clifton Chadwick called signals at quarterback, with Charles “Chas” Bennett and Fred MacKenzie at halfback and Harriman at fullback. Winchester’s account singled out MacKenzie after its 11–0 win over Saugus.

Stanley’s team faced an unusual test at Everett on October 25. The local report says Saugus had substitutes at all but two positions. On November 14 the team traveled to play the Tufts freshman eleven, the college’s Class of 1905. The Tufts report preserves the Saugus lineup from that 23–0 game.

Arthur L. William · 1914

William coached Saugus High when the school brought football back in 1914. A preseason newspaper column described it as the first team the school had fielded in several years. Freshman captain Michael Garofano played halfback, John Fox lined up at fullback, and George Sprague was among the players who helped reestablish the program.

The Warriors shut out Ipswich Manning High 14–0. A November game against Watertown ended in dispute: Saugus led 6–3 when the referee whistled a Watertown run dead for movement before the snap, and Watertown declined to continue. On Thanksgiving morning at Needham, both teams made frequent use of the forward pass. Garofano scored Saugus’s touchdown in a 38–6 loss.

Charles T. Senay · 1915–1916

Senay, a Trinity College graduate listed on the Saugus faculty, coached the Warriors in their second and third seasons after football returned. His 1915 team beat Winchester 13–6 and Dedham 14–7. Captain Michael Garofano scored in both games, twice in the mud against Dedham.

Against East Boston, fullback John Fox and quarterback John Flynn scored in a 12–10 win. The fourth quarter began after dusk, and time ran out with East Boston on the Saugus five. An injury later cost Flynn the last two games, but his teammates elected him captain for 1916. Surviving reports account for six wins, including a reported forfeit, and one loss in 1915. The season archive credits Flynn’s 1916 team with a 7–1 finish.

Edward Collier · 1917

The Lynn Item identified Edward R. Collier as the faculty coach in 1917. Graduations and wartime enlistments left him just five veterans at the opening practices. Elected captain Michael Garofano was already serving in France, so the players prepared to choose a replacement. Collier had a lighter team than the year before and intended to develop its speed. The expected return of a veteran named Betts offered some help.

Collier and student manager Roger Atherton worked to find an early opponent after a Lynn Classical date was postponed. Their preseason schedule called for trips to Dedham, Winchester, Woburn and Gloucester, with Dummer Academy at home and Chelsea scheduled to visit Saugus on Thanksgiving. The archive credits Collier’s team with five losses. The preseason clipping establishes the planned opponents only.

Laurence F. Davis · 1919

Laurence Frederic Davis graduated from Saugus High in 1917. Just two years later, at age 19, the School Board chose him to coach its football team. The appointment notice named two former Saugus athletes, George E. Sprague and Chester S. Nickerson, as his assistants. With Malcolm MacVicar as captain, the Warriors recorded three wins, six losses and three ties in 1919.

Davis later served the schools from the other side of the board table. After earning his law degree at Boston University in 1924, he practiced in Saugus, served on the School Committee and worked as tax counsel for the town.

Fred Beresford · 1920–1922, 1928

In 1922, Beresford had about 30 candidates for a team that the Daily Evening Item said averaged just 142 pounds. He used a shift to get his runners outside and gave fullback Franklin “Ooze” Pike room to run, pass and drop-kick. Against Wellesley, captain Edward “Googie” Amery broke a 95-yard run in the final minute of a scoreless game. Pike finished the drive for a 6–0 victory.

A reported 7,000 spectators came to Stackpole Field to see the Warriors hold the heavier Coburn Classical Institute team from Maine to a scoreless tie. Saugus beat Danvers 24–6 on Thanksgiving and, two days later, beat Woburn 20–9 to finish 9–0–2. Contemporary coverage described it as the school’s first unbeaten football season.

Beresford also helped establish physical education in the Saugus schools. In 1922 the School Committee unanimously chose the Lynn coach to head its new department for junior and senior high students. After coaching at Lynn’s General Electric Trade School, he returned to Saugus in 1928 to teach algebra and physical education and coach captain Reggie Goss’s team. Albion Rice took over again the following year.

William Bangs · 1923–1925

William P. Bangs took over from Fred Beresford in 1923. Captain Lester Pool and Edward “Googie” Amery, both members of the unbeaten 1922 backfield, returned for his first team. The Warriors went 2–4–3 that fall and 3–5–2 in 1924. An account of the 1924 season credits the Citizens’ Athletic Alliance with helping supply equipment.

In 1925, captain Ernest Belliveau led a team that included Amery, Homer McNutt, Wally Gautreau and Danton Sawyer. Saugus won six games that fall, matching the number it lost and giving Bangs his best finish in three seasons.

The school listed the University of Maine as Bangs’s college. As Saugus’s physical supervisor, he asked the schools to give children an extra ten to fifteen minutes of physical education each day. In his final year he also asked the town to equip and maintain summer playgrounds at Stackpole Field and Cliftondale.

Albion Rice · 1926–1927, 1929–1934

Albion Ramsdell Rice came to Saugus from a multisport background. At Bates College, the Lubec, Maine, native played football for his class team. He also played three seasons of varsity baseball and competed in two seasons of varsity track, earning letters in both sports. The 1920 Bates yearbook lists him on the college’s Athletic Council.

Rice taught algebra and supervised athletics in Saugus. His 1927 Warriors finished 7–4 behind captain John “Jack” Leahy, who later became the school’s head football coach. In his report that year, Rice called for a better football field and an indoor place to play basketball. Fred Beresford coached the 1928 team while Rice was away. The School Committee recorded Rice’s return in 1929.

Captain George Riley led Rice’s 1931 Eagles to a 7–2 record in the season archive. Against Hamilton at Stackpole Field, Harry Gates scored four touchdowns in a 52–0 win. On Thanksgiving, fullback Edward Oljey finished three long drives with touchdowns as Saugus beat Mission High of Roxbury 18–7. A generation later, Rice’s son Malcolm “Mal” Rice played on John Janusas’s 1953 North Shore champions and co-captained the 1954 team with Jack Lawrence.

Jack Leahy · 1935–1936

John “Jack” Leahy played fullback and captained Saugus under Albion Rice. A contemporary profile described how he spent his summers delivering blocks of ice, work that helped condition him for the backfield. He led Rice’s seven-win 1927 team and graduated in 1928. Saugus High inducted him into its Athletic Hall of Fame in 1992.

Leahy came back as head coach in 1935. The team won seven of ten varsity games, outscored opponents 108–91 and beat Danvers 18–12 on Thanksgiving. In 1936 his team shut out Barnstable 18–0 and Amesbury 20–0. The school adopted the Sachems nickname during his two seasons as coach.

Charles "Buzz" Harvey · 1937–1940

Harvey and assistant Nicholas Morris took a small 1939 squad led by end Stan Green and guard Jim Duffy from a 12–0 opening loss to Medford to a 9–1–1 finish and the North Shore championship. The Sachems shut out seven opponents. Against Salem, John “Iron Mike” Harrington held for Joseph Bucchiere’s field goal in a 3–0 win. Saugus closed with a 38–6 Thanksgiving victory over Danvers. The town report ranked the Sachems second among 51 Class C teams, and the School Committee rewarded the players with a five-day trip to New York City.

Before he came to Saugus, Harvey played tackle on offense and defense at Holy Cross. He made All-East three times, earned All-America mention as a junior and captained the 1934 team. He also played three seasons of varsity baseball and earned a Philadelphia Phillies tryout. He signed with the New York Giants after college, then chose to coach before playing for the club.

Harvey coached at Northbridge before Saugus and moved to Nashua High in 1941. At Nashua he won more than 200 football games and 11 New Hampshire state championships, then served as athletic director. Holy Cross inducted him into its Hall of Fame for football and baseball in 1970.

Henry "Hammering Hank" Toczylowski · 1941–1942 · Massachusetts Coaches HOF 1973

A Lynn Classical graduate, Henry “Hammering Hank” Toczylowski played quarterback in Frank Leahy’s backfield at Boston College and earned his nickname for his physical blocking. He played in the 1940 Cotton Bowl against Clemson and captained the 1940 Eagles to an 11–0 season. With the 1941 Sugar Bowl tied 13–13 late in the game, Toczylowski called the play that sent Charlie O’Rourke into the end zone for a 19–13 win over Tennessee. The New York Sun named Toczylowski to its 1940 first-team All-America backfield. He graduated from Boston College in 1941 and succeeded Buzz Harvey at Saugus after the NFL’s Brooklyn Dodgers selected him 78th overall.

Before the 1941 opener, Boston Globe columnist Harold Kaese reported that Toczylowski had turned down a $6,200 offer from Brooklyn to honor his $2,200 agreement to coach and teach English in Saugus. Nearly 7,000 watched his debut under the lights against Chelsea. Eddie Shipulski scored twice and threw a touchdown pass, and co-captain John “Iron Mike” Harrington scored as both a runner and a passer in a 32–0 win.

Toczylowski and assistant Edward Galligan installed a new system and led Saugus to the 1941 Class C state championship. The town report records a 7–3–1 finish in 1942. As reported in William P. Coughlin’s Boston Globe obituary, Toczylowski later coached Arlington High to a state championship and spent more than 30 years there, teaching business courses and serving as a house dean. He was inducted into the Boston College Varsity Club Hall of Fame.

Dave Lucey · 1943–1946 · Saugus HOF 1994

Lucey played quarterback at Boston College from 1937 through 1939. When his teammate Henry “Hammering Hank” Toczylowski was injured in November 1939, Lucey stepped in as the starter. That Boston College team went on to play Clemson in the 1940 Cotton Bowl.

Lucey succeeded Toczylowski at Saugus in 1943, when Doug Waybright captained the team. A year later, captain Art Spinney earned first-team Boston Globe All-Scholastic honors at end as the Sachems won all ten regular-season games. They outscored their opponents 207–19 and recorded eight shutouts. A reported 14,000 saw Saugus beat Lynn Classical 24–13 at Manning Bowl. The Sachems clinched the Class B championship on Thanksgiving with a 26–0 win at Wakefield. They later faced Class A champion Peabody in a separate postseason game, which Peabody won 19–7.

In 1947, after four seasons at Saugus, Lucey returned to Boston College as an assistant football coach. The following year he became the athletic department’s first full-time public relations director. He later coached high school football at St. Mary’s of Lynn, Peabody and St. John’s Prep.

John Janusas · 1947–1960 · Saugus HOF 1992 · Massachusetts Coaches HOF 1970

Janusas took over in 1947 and went 7–3 in his first season. He worked with assistant coach John “Tex” Scarborough, who went on to lead the Saugus athletic department. In 1949, co-captains Salvatore “Sardi” Nicolo and Dan Murphy led the Sachems to nine straight wins to close the year. They shared the North Shore championship with Gloucester and beat Weymouth 7–6 in the Exchange Bowl. Richie Nicolo threw the tying 50-yard touchdown pass to his brother Sardi, and Leo Kane kicked the winning point. Sportswriter Harvey L. Southward named Janusas and Gloucester’s Nate Ross his area coaches of the year.

The Sachems repeated as Exchange Bowl champions in 1950, beating Brockton 30–12 behind Richie Nicolo’s two touchdown passes, scoring run and interception return for a score. Janusas rebuilt again in 1953. With all but one regular back from a 1–9 team, Saugus won the North Shore League. His 1959 team shared the league title with Salem. He also coached Saugus boys hockey to North Shore Hockey League titles in 1947–48 and 1948–49.

At Boston College, Janusas played tackle for nearly three seasons without coming off the field during games. He earned three All-New England selections, made All-East in 1937 and received All-America recognition. He also played defense on BC’s 1938 New England championship hockey team. After college, he faced the New York Giants as a College All-Star and joined Gil Dobie’s BC football staff as an assistant in 1938. He played professional tackle for the Providence Steam Roller from 1939 through 1941 and started a game for the Hartford Blues in 1941. Boston College inducted him into its Varsity Club Hall of Fame in 1995.

Bernard Stead · 1961–1963

Bernard “Bernie” Stead attended Lynn Classical and lettered in football at the University of Massachusetts in 1942, 1946 and 1947. Marine Corps service in the Pacific during World War II interrupted his college career. He returned to UMass after the war and also boxed, winning the Chelsea Athletic Club championship in 1946.

Stead succeeded John Janusas at Saugus in 1961. Eric Wormstead and Dick Allen captained his first team, which also featured Tom Carr. John Driscoll captained the 1962 team, which earned the only victory of Stead’s three seasons. The Sachems came within a touchdown of Peabody that Thanksgiving, falling 12–6. Stead also coached baseball and basketball and taught history, mathematics and physical education. His family remembered that he marked a Saugus football win by lighting a cigar.

Walter Sheridan · 1964–1966 · Massachusetts Coaches HOF 1974

Sheridan came to Saugus from Salem High after a playing career at Holy Cross. In 1946 he won the Eddie O’Melia Trophy as the outstanding player in the Holy Cross–Boston College game. At Saugus his first team, captained by Ronnie Barressi, beat Peabody 26–13 on Thanksgiving in 1964. The Sachems beat the Tanners again in 1965, this time 35–12 behind three Mike Kimball touchdowns.

By 1966, quarterback Christie “Chris” Serino, halfbacks Jim Paolini and Kimball, and fullback Ed Maguire had played together for three seasons. Sheridan used a pro-style offense that made it difficult to concentrate on one runner. After Maguire gained 150 yards against Boston College High, Beverly keyed on him. Serino used the passing game to set up Paolini, who scored three times in a 36–8 win. Saugus opened 5–0 and finished 7–2, scoring 277 points and allowing 74. Maguire, tackle John Dancewicz and Paul Uga were the co-captains.

Paul Maguire · 1967–1972

Maguire coached Saugus for six seasons beginning in 1967. His 1968 team had scored just 32 points through five games, then scored 32 in one afternoon against Swampscott. Three wins followed. On Thanksgiving the Sachems held previously unbeaten Peabody at the one-yard line early in the second half and went on to win. Co-captains Jim Canole and Lou O’Neil earned Lynn Item recognition at utility and on the offensive line, respectively.

That finish carried into 1969. Captains Bill Babcock, Richard Conti and John Richard led Saugus to five straight opening wins and a 7–2 finish. In the 29–6 opener against Boston College High, Dan Heaney scored on runs of 82 and 61 yards and Richard rushed for 128 yards. Quarterback Bob Osgood and Bill Eichel earned first-team NEC honors on offense, and Steve Nelson made the first team on defense. The only losses came against eventual class champions Swampscott and Peabody.

Mike Ginolfi · 1973–1983, 1994–1995 · Saugus HOF 1993 · Massachusetts Coaches HOF 1992

Mike “Gino” Ginolfi came to Saugus with a state championship already to his credit. A Stamford, Connecticut, native, he earned a full football scholarship to Northeastern. The Huskies list him as a varsity letterwinner in 1962 and 1964. After graduating in 1965, he taught physical education and coached football in Keene, New Hampshire, where his team won the 1970 state championship.

His first Saugus team went 3–6 in 1973 and closed by beating previously unbeaten Peabody 12–0. Two years later, captains Tom Heaney, Doug Mackie, John Nicolo and Steve Gannon led a 9–1 team that shared the Northeastern Conference title with Salem. In their head-to-head meeting, Saugus erased a 14–0 halftime deficit. Jay LeBranti found Frank Scuzzarella on third-and-19 late in the winning drive, and the Sachems prevailed 15–14.

In 1977, co-captains Dan Hashem and Frank Doyle led another 9–1 team, this time winning the NEC outright. George Nicolo threw four touchdown passes at Beverly, two each to John Coffey and Frank Serino. Saugus won its final five games, and the Daily Evening Item named Ginolfi its Coach of the Year.

Ginolfi followed John “Tex” Scarborough as Saugus athletic director and served until he retired in 2002. The 1984 yearbook lists Ginolfi as director. His obituary dates his start to 1982, while Scarborough’s retirement coverage places the handoff after the 1982–83 school year. After his first coaching tenure ended in 1983, former players and assistants went on to lead the program. Ginolfi himself returned in 1994. His 1995 team snapped a 34-game losing streak with a 25–0 win over Winthrop. Mike Mabee threw the opening touchdown to Derek Bilton, L.J. McKanas ran for 178 yards, and the defense twice stopped Winthrop inside the Saugus 10. It was the start of the school’s first winning season since 1990.

Kevin Ward · 1984–1990 · Saugus HOF 2001

Ward spent 14 years as an assistant before his first game as Saugus head coach in 1984. That opener took him to Winthrop’s Miller Field, where the Vikings held the longest unbeaten streak in Massachusetts. Saugus won 15–7, ending the streak at 33 games.

Ward’s 1987 Sachems finished 7–3 and beat Peabody 21–20 on Thanksgiving. Co-captain Joe Barressi caught nine touchdown passes, returned two punts for scores and earned Lynn Item honors at defensive back. Fellow captain Larry Furey earned Item honors on the offensive line. On Thanksgiving in 1988, Marc Fauci ran for two touchdowns and Steve Worthley caught six passes for 92 yards in a 22–14 win over Peabody, as recorded in Bob Sacco’s Saugus Advertiser illustration. Peabody edged Saugus 14–13 the following Thanksgiving. Fauci earned Boston Globe All-Scholastic recognition in 1989.

In 1990, tri-captains Ryan Magnasco, Dan Schena and Chris Mikulski led Ward’s final team to an 8–2 finish. Saugus scored 122 points in its first three wins. Justin Barrett scored five touchdowns against Gloucester, three of them in the opening quarter. The ten final scores total 284 points, which the Globe reported as a school record at the time.

Mike Bontempo · 1991–1993

Mike “Bonty” Bontempo succeeded Kevin Ward in 1991. His first team went 1–9 and played Peabody to within a touchdown on Thanksgiving, 21–14. Senior captain Sean Publicover earned both Northeastern Conference and Lynn Item recognition for his work on the defensive line.

Chad Parks captained Bontempo’s 1992 team. Joe Attubato, already among that squad’s notable players, became a captain in 1993. Like Publicover, Attubato earned NEC and Item honors as a senior defensive lineman. The 1992 and 1993 teams each finished 0–10. Mike Ginolfi returned to the program the following fall.

John Fauci · 1996–1998

Fauci grew up in Peabody and began teaching in Saugus in 1971. He earned degrees at Suffolk University and Boston State College, coached the Sachems as an assistant, and succeeded Mike Ginolfi as head coach in 1996. His first team featured L.J. McKanas, the NEC Offensive Player of the Year. Against Salem, quarterback Ryan Leahy found McKanas on an 89-yard scoring pass, and McKanas threw a 64-yard touchdown to Dominic Zammit.

Fauci’s 1997 team finished 6–4–1. Dominic and Raphael Zammit combined for 251 rushing yards in the opener against Greater Lawrence. Later that season Leahy found Chris McBride for the winning touchdown in a 24–20 victory at Salem. In 1998 Raphael rushed for five touchdowns against Winthrop and four against Marblehead. Fauci went on to serve as Saugus High’s assistant athletic director and finished his career in the school district as principal of Waybright Elementary.

Dean Sacca · 1999–2000

Sacca’s first game as Saugus head coach stayed scoreless until the fourth quarter, when sophomore quarterback Kyle Sucharewicz ran in the touchdown that beat Methuen 6–0. Senior co-captains George Lowe and Brian Mirasolo led the 1999 team. Lowe scored three times in a 30–14 win over Marblehead and caught a 65-yard touchdown pass from Sucharewicz against Swampscott.

In 2000 the captains were Ryan Gould, Adam Magnasco and Billy Robinson. At Swampscott, Pat Quinlan scored both Saugus touchdowns, breaking a 10–7 halftime deficit with a 60-yard run in a 13–10 win. Sacca later spent nine years as offensive coordinator at Belmont before taking over as interim head coach during the 2013 season.

John Nicolo · 2001–2004 · Saugus HOF 1987

Before he coached the Sachems, Nicolo captained Mike Ginolfi’s 1975 NEC co-champions alongside Tom Heaney, Doug Mackie and Steve Gannon. He went on to letter four years in both football and baseball at Ithaca. An All-American running back and captain on the 1979 Division III football champions, he finished his college football career with 1,817 rushing yards and 20 touchdowns. The following spring he played shortstop on Ithaca’s national championship baseball team and earned Division III World Series MVP honors. He spent a year in the Houston Astros’ minor league organization. Ithaca inducted him into its Athletic Hall of Fame in 1986.

As Saugus head coach in 2001, Nicolo saw his team erase a 14–0 deficit at Salem and win 30–14. Kyle Sucharewicz threw three touchdown passes and Dan Landry returned an interception 55 yards for another score. His son John “Sal” Nicolo played fullback and linebacker on the 2001 and 2002 teams. Sal’s 35-yard fumble return set up the first Saugus touchdown in a 31–16 win over Marblehead in 2002.

In 2003 his younger son Mike set the school’s then-single-game rushing record with 306 yards against Salem. A week later Mike ran for 241 yards and all four Saugus touchdowns in a 29–14 win at Beverly. He went on to captain the 2004 team with Brian Bianchi and Rob Gannon. John stepped down after that season to become athletic director.

Michael Broderick · 2005–2016

Broderick taught history and sociology at Saugus and coached football for twelve seasons. He played college baseball at Suffolk University and coached football at Arlington High and Curry College. His 2006 Sachems beat Peabody on Thanksgiving. A year later his triple-option offense helped deliver the school’s first winning football season since 1997.

The 2007 Sachems averaged 280 rushing yards per game and closed with three road wins, including a 49–14 Thanksgiving victory at Chelsea. The Item credits Broderick with the NEC South title and conference Coach of the Year honors that season. He coached in the 2008 Agganis All-Star game. That fall Saugus went 7–3 for the second straight year, with Mike Dean rushing for 1,242 yards and quarterback Brett Reid adding 800. Reid later returned as one of Broderick’s assistants.

Broderick also coached winning teams in 2012 and 2014. In 2014 Dan Cacciola ran for 375 yards and four touchdowns against Winthrop, surpassing Mike Nicolo’s earlier single-game rushing mark. One of Broderick’s former varsity players, Greg Bluestein, became Saugus head coach in 2026.

Anthony Nalen · 2017

Nalen played quarterback at Pope John XXIII High School in Everett and at Springfield College, where he helped the 2006 team win the Empire 8 championship. Pope John inducted him into its athletic hall of fame. Before his Saugus appointment, Nalen led Bishop Connolly to an undefeated Mayflower Athletic Conference season in 2015 and earned conference Coach of the Year honors.

His 2017 Sachems improved from one win the previous fall to four. After a one-point loss at Gloucester, Saugus beat Winthrop 40–13. Later the team won three straight against Pentucket, Amesbury and Greater Lowell Tech. Ricky Martinez earned both NEC and Lynn Item honors. Nalen’s Saugus tenure ended before the 2018 season. His subsequent coaching career included an appointment as head coach at Bishop Guertin in Nashua, New Hampshire.

Mike Mabee · 2018 · Saugus HOF 2006

Mabee was a senior co-captain and two-way player on Ginolfi’s 1995 team. He threw the opening touchdown to Derek Bilton in the 25–0 win over Winthrop that ended a 34-game losing streak. Later, in the win over Lynn English that clinched a winning season, he threw two touchdown passes, ran for a score and intercepted two passes. He earned Agganis All-Star selections in both football and baseball. At Plymouth State he appeared on the football roster as a defensive back in 1996 and a quarterback in 1997.

In 2018 Mabee returned to his alma mater as interim head coach. His team shut out Salem 14–0 and beat Pentucket 21–7 on the way to a 4–7 finish. Dante McGrane earned NEC and Lynn Item All-Star recognition. The Saugus Athletic Hall of Fame had inducted Mabee in 2006 for his playing career.

Steve Cummings · 2019–2025

Cummings played football at Framingham State and later served on its coaching staff. He also worked as an assistant at Canton, Sharon, Salem and Haverhill and as head coach at Wakefield before taking the Saugus job in 2019. Marvens Jean earned All-Star recognition on his first Sachems team. Cummings then guided the program through a pandemic-shortened seven-game season.

His 2023 team opened 3–0 and finished 5–6. Senior Braden Faiella made 140 tackles and earned Lynn Item Defensive Player of the Year honors. In Cummings’s final season, Saugus shut out East Boston 34–0. On Thanksgiving at Peabody, the Sachems recovered a blocked punt in the end zone and led 20–0 five minutes into the game before the Tanners rallied for a 28–26 win. His defensive coordinator, Greg Bluestein, succeeded him in 2026.

Greg Bluestein · 2026–present

Bluestein began playing in Saugus Pop Warner at age seven, played four years of varsity football for Mike Broderick and graduated from Saugus High in 2008. He started coaching as a volunteer at Everett in 2012. Over a decade there he worked his way up to defensive coordinator, taking that job in 2018. He also served as president of Saugus Youth Football & Cheer.

Bluestein returned to the Sachems as defensive coordinator in 2022 and became Steve Cummings’s associate head coach in 2025. Saugus named him head coach in February 2026. His first team, captained by Paxton Ferraro and Pharoah Brandenburg, forfeited two October games because of roster shortages. The players returned to the field at Arlington Catholic. On November 13 they beat O’Bryant 24–20 for Bluestein’s first victory as Saugus head coach.

Assistant Coaches & Athletic Leadership

John A. “Tex” Scarborough

Scarborough joined the Saugus schools in 1947 as a teacher and assistant football coach under John Janusas. He was on the staff when the Sachems won the 1953 North Shore League title. He also coached baseball and led Saugus High’s boys basketball team for ten years.

Appointed director of athletics and physical education in 1961, Scarborough served in the role until his retirement in 1983. The Northeastern Conference athletic directors named him Man of the Year for 1978–79. About 300 former players, coaches, colleagues, and friends attended his retirement testimonial. Mike Ginolfi praised Scarborough for putting Saugus students first. Saugus High inducted him into its Athletic Hall of Fame as a coach and athletic director in 1988.

The farewell clipping for Janusas identifies Scarborough as Saugus’s incoming athletic director and names Lexington as the school where Janusas would coach football and direct athletics. The Item’s retirement coverage describes Scarborough as Saugus’s first athletic director. Ginolfi succeeded him, and the 1984 Saugus yearbook lists Ginolfi as director.

05 / The long view

A brief history of Saugus football.

Contributed research · The narrative below follows the supplied document, with coach spelling, Dean Sacca’s 1999–2000 tenure, and Exchange Bowl details corrected by Sal Nicolo. Names, dates, statistics, and time-sensitive statements remain subject to review.

The Item’s holiday retrospective credits Saugus with 16 straight Thanksgiving seasons without a loss from 1929 through 1944, against Mission High, Danvers and Wakefield. Peabody first met Saugus in a separate 1944 postseason game at Manning Bowl. The annual rivalry’s early dates vary across later accounts: Dave Rattigan described the 1945 and 1946 meetings as spring games and put the start of the Thanksgiving series in 1947, while other sources place earlier meetings around the holiday.

About the Site’s Researcher

Saugus Footballer Researcher

Sal Nicolo has been around Sachem football his whole life. His grandfather, Salvatore “Sardi” Nicolo, and his great-uncle Richie Nicolo played together on Saugus’s 1949 Exchange Bowl championship team. His father, John, helped the Sachems win a share of the 1975 Northeastern Conference title and later returned to coach. His father’s cousin George played quarterback, his uncle Pete played receiver, and his brother Mike was a running back and team captain. Sardi, Richie, John, George, and Mike are all members of the Saugus High School Athletic Hall of Fame.

Those family ties run through the brothers’ names, too. The site’s researcher, John “Sal” Nicolo, was named for his father, John, and his grandfather, Salvatore. Sal’s brother, Michael Stephen Nicolo, was named for Coach Mike Ginolfi and Steve Gannon. In 1994 and 1995, Ginolfi gave Sal a place on the Sachems’ sideline as the team’s towel boy. Through his father and his years around the program, Sal got to know former Sachem players and coaches including Mike Ginolfi, Pat Petrone, Rich Salerno, Steve Gannon, Mike Bontempo, John Fauci, Dean Sacca, Tim Fawcett, Donnie Trainor, and Tommy Trainor.

When Sal took the field for Saugus High, he wore his father’s No. 1 as a freshman and sophomore, then his grandfather’s No. 33 as a junior and senior. He played fullback, defensive back, and linebacker, starting at fullback and linebacker for his father in 2001 and 2002. A 2003 Saugus High graduate, Sal was selected for the Harry Agganis All-Star Football Classic and went on to play linebacker at Bridgewater State.

Sal created this site out of love and respect for Sachem football and the people who shaped it. His hope is that a former player can find a teammate’s name, a family can rediscover a game it remembers, and a current Sachem can learn who wore the uniform before them.
