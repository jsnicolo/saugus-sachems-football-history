# Notable alumni source review — November 8, 2026

Reviewed all 58 existing profiles; expanded or corrected 50. Eight were retained where additional evidence was insufficient. No new alumni or graduation-year changes were made.

## Sources and method

Cross-referenced the website’s 399 gallery entries (478 unique images), season records, honors and college directory; the contributor’s Football(2).zip collection (390 source images), image/OCR review inventory, player-stat review CSVs, historical workbook and narrative, previous alumni research, and relevant newer source-only clippings. Searchable inventories served as discovery aids; substantive new game details were checked against the original clippings/yearbooks. College, conference and professional claims were checked against institutional records where available. This is a profile-by-profile source review, not a claim that every unrelated image has been individually re-transcribed.

Individual biographies link the evidence for their additions. No projected college destination was treated as college participation; projected All-Star starters and injured nonparticipants are identified precisely. Historical records are described as records at the time when appropriate.

## Material corrections and open questions

- Dan Murphy’s 1949 Globe distinction is a reserve selection.
- Richie Nicolo’s 1951 Boston College letter is documented; the prior LSU playing claim lacks roster verification and was omitted.
- John Nicolo’s 1979 football All-America award is specified as Associated Press second team. His HOF career-yardage figure remains attributed to that historical biography because later official totals differ.
- Curry’s first NCAA playoff victory was against Hartwick in 2007. Mike Nicolo recorded three tackles in the following round at St. John Fisher; an official 2008 preview says his shoulder injury had kept him out that season. The later Ithaca win is described as a team achievement.
- Justin Winn’s 2013 Item capsule reads 1,103 rushing yards; the prior 1,108 interpretation was corrected.
- Braden Faiella’s 482 career tackles are documented; a possible state record was not upgraded to a confirmed record.
- Retained contributor-confirmed class years for Edward Amery and Michael Garofano; early chronology questions remain unresolved.
- Jim Mancuso’s intended UConn destination remains separate from verified college participation. Eddie Maguire’s Indiana letters, 34 tackles for loss and 1970 second-team All-Big Ten honor are documented by Indiana’s record book.
